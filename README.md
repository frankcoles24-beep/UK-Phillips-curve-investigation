# UK-Phillips-curve-investigation
Small research project into whether or not the Phillips relationship between inflation and unemplopyment holds according to historical data from the ONS spanning 1989-2026. 

Methodology:
UK inflation and unemployment data was input into R to observe trends over time as well as measure the strength of the relationship between unemployment and inflation, the relationship across different time periods and the relationship between unemployment and the change in inflation

Results:
across the whole time series the relationship did not hold, instead observing a weak, statistically insignificant positive correlation for both the inflation and change of inflation relationship to unemployment. However, there was a much stronger relationship in particular periods of the UK history.

Summative Conclusions:
The data is too simple to fully analyse the Phillips relationship, and other forces are likely influencing unemployment and inflation in ways beyond what the Phillips curve relationship can encompass, however the strength of the relationship may be more pronounced depending on the individual economic circumstances of the given time period.
